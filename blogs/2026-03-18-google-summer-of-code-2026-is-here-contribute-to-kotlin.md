<p>The Kotlin Foundation is joining <a href="https://summerofcode.withgoogle.com/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Google Summer of Code (GSoC) 2026</a>! If you are a student or an eligible contributor looking to spend your summer working on a real-world open-source project, this is your chance to make a meaningful impact on the Kotlin ecosystem while also benefiting from the mentorship of experienced engineers.</p>

<h1 class="wp-block-heading">Why participate?</h1>



<p>GSoC is one of the best-known programs for introducing new contributors to open-source development. By joining, you&#8217;ll work on a real project, get hands-on guidance from a dedicated mentor, earn a stipend, and come away with production experience. Last year’s contributors shipped work that is already being used by other developers, from a compiler-integrated Kotlin LSP to KMP support for Firebase AI. They wrote about how the program changed their perspective on open-source development – you can<a href="https://blog.jetbrains.com/kotlin/2025/11/google-summer-of-code-2025/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank"> read their stories here.</a></p>



<h1 class="wp-block-heading">This year’s projects:</h1>



<ul>
<li><strong>Kotlin Compiler Fuzzer (Kai)</strong> – Build a new, modular fuzzer for the Kotlin compiler from scratch. [Hard, 350 hrs]</li>



<li><strong>Swift-to-Kotlin interop (PoC)</strong> – Create a proof of concept for importing Swift APIs into Kotlin/Native. [Hard, 350 hrs]</li>



<li><strong>Tail call support in Kotlin/Wasm</strong> – Design and implement tail call support in the Kotlin/Wasm backend. [Medium, 90 hrs]</li>



<li><strong>Kotlin Education landscape report</strong> – Research and document how Kotlin is taught worldwide, creating reusable datasets and strategic input. [Medium, 175 hrs]</li>
</ul>



<p>Browse all <a href="http://kotlinlang.org/docs/gsoc-2026.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">project details and contributor guidelines here</a>.</p>



<h1 class="wp-block-heading">Important dates:</h1>



<ul>
<li><strong>March 16</strong> – Contributor application period opens</li>



<li><strong>March 31</strong> – Application deadline</li>



<li><strong>April 30</strong> – Accepted projects announced</li>



<li><strong>May 25</strong> – Coding begins!</li>
</ul>



<h1 class="wp-block-heading">How to apply:</h1>



<ol>
<li>Explore the <a href="https://kotlinlang.org/docs/gsoc-2026.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">project ideas</a> and pick one that interests you.</li>



<li>Join the<a href="https://slack-chats.kotlinlang.org/c/gsoc" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank"> #gsoc channel on Kotlin Slack</a> to connect with mentors and ask questions.</li>



<li>Review the<a href="https://kotlinlang.org/docs/gsoc-overview.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank"> contributor guidelines</a> and prepare a code sample.</li>



<li><a href="https://summerofcode.withgoogle.com/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Submit your application</a> on the GSoC website by March 31.</li>
</ol>



<p>Questions? You can reach us on Slack or gsoc@kotlinfoundation.org.</p>



<h1 class="wp-block-heading">Not a student?</h1>



<p>If you are an open-source maintainer or contributor but not eligible for GSoC, the<a href="https://blog.jetbrains.com/kotlin/2026/02/kotlin-ecosystem-mentorship/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank"> Kotlin Ecosystem Mentorship Program</a> may be for you. The current round is already underway, but we are likely to run another one – follow our announcements so you don’t miss it.</p>



<p>Join us this summer, we look forward to your proposals!</p>
